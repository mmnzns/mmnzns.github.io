## 01. Identity Anchor

**The one-liner:**

I get handed a problem, not a spec. I read what's actually wrong, decide what should exist, choose the metric that proves it, and build the system that gets there.

**Career Evolution Arc**

Copywriting → Email → Lifecycle → Web → SEO → CRO → Martech → Automation → AI Systems

This is not a career pivot. It is natural evolution driven by following the problem upstream. Each stage built the exact capability needed to see and solve the next problem in the GTM system. Lifecycle is the through-line connecting every stage. The arc's endpoint is AI automation infrastructure, where lifecycle, behavioral data, and triggers run as intelligent systems.

**Operating Philosophy**

- Define the real problem before building the solution. Most requests are symptoms, not the actual issue.
- Identify what is missing, not just what is broken. The gap is usually upstream from where people are looking.
- Build systems that work without me in the room. If it only works when I am there, it is not a system.

**Origin and Background**

Growing up on a farm shaped the way I naturally approach problems.

On a farm, you learn very quickly that you either work with what you have or build what you need. Problems rarely have a single clean solution. You learn to observe, adapt, troubleshoot, and think creatively with real-world constraints in front of you.

As a kid, I learned carpentry, how to repair broken tools and appliances, and how to anticipate failure before it happened. If a storm was coming and the roof was weak, reinforcing it was not optional. The consequences were real. That mindset stayed with me.

Coupled with a natural curiosity about how things worked, and parents who constantly encouraged learning, that environment shaped my builder mentality early. If they did not know the answer to one of my questions, they would help me find books or resources so I could learn it myself.

Systems thinking was part of daily life long before it became a professional skill.

That perspective still shows up in how I build today. I naturally gravitate toward long-term reliability over short-term fixes, and toward work that changes how people operate over time, not just how they click.

That mindset carried into my first professional role at Rappler, where I trained under Nobel Peace Prize laureate Maria Ressa in a 24/7 digital newsroom. Working in that environment developed precision, fact-based thinking, and a refusal to accept surface-level explanations when the root cause was still unclear.

That editorial discipline still influences how I frame problems, validate information, and document systems today.

Later, at Sportserve, being placed in the middle of multiple operational functions shaped the operating style that still defines my work.

I was involved across website development, lifecycle operations, payment systems, integrations, SOP development, vendor coordination, project management, and team building. Not inside one specialty, but between specialties.

That forced me to understand how every part of the operation connected.

Over time, it trained the instinct that has defined my career ever since: follow the problem upstream, learn whatever is necessary to solve it, and build something that prevents the issue from repeating.

That is ultimately why my roles continue evolving across lifecycle, web operations, martech, automation, and AI systems. I do not see them as isolated disciplines. I see them as connected parts of the same operational system.

The strongest work, in my experience, happens where systems thinking, empathy, curiosity, and real-world impact intersect.

One belief has stayed constant throughout all of it: learning is a lifelong pursuit.

I do not believe in “I can’t do this.” I believe in “I haven’t learned it yet.”

One symbol that represents that philosophy for me is the West African Adinkra symbol “Nea Onnim No Sua A, Ohu,” which translates to: “He who does not know can know from learning.”

It symbolizes lifelong education, curiosity, and the continuous pursuit of knowledge. I have it tattooed on my right wrist as a reminder that most challenges are not impossible. They simply require learning, patience, and persistence.

### CAR #12: Intelligent Investing Winback and Cross-Sell Program at Mogo

**Approach 1: The Map**

Miguel designed the program as a multi-iteration system rather than a single campaign. Pre-pruning would have removed salvageable contacts based on guesswork. Iteration 1 would reveal the truth.

Two behavioral hooks anchored the program throughout: an investment calculator for the Manage path (a projection tool where users modeled what a small weekly contribution could grow to by age 75) and [Fiscal.ai](http://Fiscal.ai) for the Self-directed path (a research tool already available in every Mogo membership).

**Approach 2: The Details**

**Iteration 1 (Feb-Jun 2025, ~900k audience):**

Wave 1 re-introduced both products and routed users toward the appropriate hook based on product fit. Manage-fit users were driven to the investment calculator to model habit-based compounding. Self-directed-fit users were introduced or reintroduced to [Fiscal.ai](http://Fiscal.ai) as the research layer for informed trading.

The cadence ran over five months: re-introduction in Month 1 with a subject-line resend to non-openers in Week 2, product-specific sequences in Months 2-3 with separate Manage and Self-directed creative tracks, objection handling in Months 3-4 using a one-click survey where users identified their barrier (too risky, cash flow issues, confusion about the products, or already on another platform), and a light taper in Months 4-5 for the coldest segments.

Each objection response was tailored: risk concern mapped to long-term DCA education and calculator reinforcement, cash flow concern mapped to micro-contribution framing ($10-20 per week), confusion mapped to clean product explainers, and the already-on-another-platform objection was repositioned as complementary rather than competitive.

Iteration 1 result: approximately 2.7% conversion from the 900k pool. After the run, Miguel culled hard bounces, spam complaints, and addresses with three or more soft bounces. The active reachable audience dropped to approximately 600k for Iteration 2.

**Iteration 2 (Jul 2025-Jan 2026, ~600k audience):**

The key structural decision in Iteration 2 was how to handle the completely unresponsive segment. Two options existed: go dark on them entirely, or continue at some frequency. Going dark risked damaging sender reputation when re-engagement was attempted later, because a sudden bulk re-engagement spike looks like a cold list to inbox providers. Continuing normal cadence would burn the list. Miguel chose a third path: a low-frequency monthly drip for never-interactors, one educational email per month, low-pressure, alternating between calculator-based content for the Manage path and [Fiscal.ai](http://Fiscal.ai) research concepts for the Self-directed path. The goal was to maintain minimum communication for deliverability health while keeping the segment warm enough that a future attempt wouldn't spike. Conversion rate among the cleaned audience was chosen as the Iteration 2 proof metric, specifically to show whether cleaning the list and refining the strategy had actually improved signal quality.

Iteration 2 result: approximately 5% conversion from the 600k cleaned audience. Roughly 30k people either activated Intelligent Investing for the first time or returned to meaningful activity. Remaining reachable audience for Iteration 3: approximately 300k.

**Result:**

Across the first two iterations, the program converted approximately 54k people from a dormant or never-invested base into active Intelligent Investing users across both Self-directed and Manage. Iteration 3 is now running against the remaining 300k, with a planned sunset program for zero-engagement remainders following the final pass.

### CAR #13: Salesforce Lifecycle Automation Suite at Sportserve (Dafabet)

**Best for:** Salesforce Marketing Cloud expertise, PLG-style lifecycle design, behavior-driven automation, regulated industry experience, cross-channel lifecycle orchestration, compliance-aware marketing systems, gaming and B2C retention

**Context:**

At Sportserve, one of the B2C gaming platforms (Dafabet) needed a structured lifecycle system to drive user activation, reduce dormancy, and deepen product usage within the constraints of safer-gambling regulations and multi-jurisdiction compliance requirements across APAC, EU, and LATAM. Users were registering but a significant portion were not placing a first bet. Existing users were going dormant. High-value users were not being identified or systematically nurtured. Cross-sport engagement was low despite a broad product offering. And everything had to operate within jurisdiction-specific promotional rules with compliant messaging baked into the system architecture, not added afterward. The read was that all of these problems were symptoms of the same underlying gap: lifecycle was being treated as a campaign layer on top of the product rather than as a behavioral system designed around the product's actual activation event. First bet was the aha moment, equivalent to a SaaS product's trial-to-paid conversion, and nothing in the existing lifecycle was designed around it.

**Approach 1: The Map**

The decision was to design six interconnected flows built outward from first bet as the primary activation milestone, rather than treating each retention or re-engagement problem as a separate campaign. First-bet conversion rate was chosen as the primary proof metric because it was the most direct measure of whether the lifecycle system was actually moving users through the activation event. Compliance was baked into the system architecture from the start, not added afterward, because retrofitting compliant messaging into an already-built system in a multi-jurisdiction environment creates legal exposure and operational debt. Salesforce Marketing Cloud was the orchestration layer throughout.

**Approach 2: The Details**

**Flow 1 - New-user onboarding (first-bet activation):** Journey Builder entry triggered from the registration event, filtering for users with no first bet within 24 hours. Cross-channel sequence: Email 1 immediately (welcome offer, simple market recommendation), mobile push or SMS one hour after signup (short tip and deep-link to the day's biggest match), Email 2 at 24 hours for still-inactive users (beginner-friendly markets and promo terms refresher).

**Flow 2 - Dormant bettor re-engagement:** ETL-based data extensions pushed last bet timestamp and account status into Salesforce. Journey Builder filtered on 7 or more days since last bet with at least one lifetime bet. Day 0 email dynamically pulled upcoming matches based on geo and sport preferences. Day 3 email surfaced a bonus incentive with a personalized promo code and countdown timer, using AMPscript to respect regional regulatory caps. Day 5 in-play SMS or push nudge linked directly to the live-match section where eligible.

**Flow 3 - High-value bettor nurturing:** Two behavioral triggers identified power users: three or more bets per week with a deposit in the last 14 days, or average bet size in the top 20% of users. VIP-style weekly email with major upcoming matches and a personal betting activity snapshot. Real-time push or in-app messages triggered on streak milestones. CRM field update tagging high-value users for account manager routing and personalized rewards where regulations allowed.

**Flow 4 - First-withdrawal trust and depth-building:** Withdrawal event pushed into Salesforce via API or data sync, triggering a confirmation email reinforcing trust and nudging toward deeper features (cash-out, accumulators, responsible gambling tools). A Salesforce-orchestrated in-app banner surfaced a full-circle player message linking to live in-play and cash-out sections.

**Flow 5 - Churn-risk and safer-gambling:** Short-term behavioral volatility signals (five or more bets in one hour, net loss thresholds) imported into Salesforce as real-time flags, triggering in-app messages and emails with time-out options and jurisdiction-pre-approved responsible gambling copy. A separate 30-plus-days-dormant journey combined low-risk promos with responsible gambling links and self-exclusion options, using segmented data extensions and AMPscript for regional compliance.

**Flow 6 - Cross-sport expansion:** Sport-usage matrix in Salesforce flagged users betting heavily in one sport but not others. Email cross-promoted an adjacent sport with a first-bet coupon and odds-based personalization. A Salesforce-powered in-app content block surfaced a sports-you-have-not-tried carousel with first-bet incentives per sport category.

**Result:**

- First-bet conversion rate: ~8% to 15% improvement after onboarding automation
- 7-day dormant user reactivation: ~10% to 20% improvement with triggered winback journeys
- CTR on triggered lifecycle campaigns: ~20% to 40% above batch campaign benchmarks
- Manual campaign setup time: reduced by ~20% to 30% through segmentation and automation

The metrics confirmed the original read: lifecycle had been treated as a campaign layer on top of the product rather than as a behavioral system built around the activation event. Once the architecture was designed around first bet as the core milestone and behavioral data drove every subsequent path, the system produced results that batch campaigns never had. The framework built here, treating a single product action as the activation milestone, building behavioral flows outward from that event, and baking compliance into the architecture rather than adding it later, directly shaped how lifecycle systems were approached at Mogo and DTC afterward.

### GTM System Definition Framework

**Define → Map → Build → Validate → Scale**

**Define:** Identify the real goal behind the request. Challenge surface-level requirements. Clarify what success actually looks like.

**Map:** Map workflows, data flow, and system dependencies. Identify gaps, inefficiencies, and manual workarounds. Define how systems should connect.

**Build:** Design and implement automation workflows. Combine deterministic logic with AI where appropriate. Ensure outputs are structured and usable.

**Validate:** Test under real operational conditions. Identify edge cases, failures, and inconsistencies. Iterate based on real usage, not assumptions.

**Scale:** Document workflows and logic. Enable teams to operate without dependency. Continuously refine systems after launch based on real behavior.

### Gap Identification Model

**The signals to look for:**

- Repeated manual work with no automation in place
- Workarounds living in Slack, spreadsheets, or side processes
- Systems that exist but are not fully used or connected
- Data that is available but not flowing where it is needed
- Delays between steps that should be instant
- Decisions being made without reliable data

**The action:**

- Trace root cause across systems, not just within the broken component
- Identify what is missing, not just what is broken
- Define the system that should exist, then build it

### Automation Design Principles

- Build for the real workflow, not the ideal workflow
- Structure outputs so teams can actually use them
- Separate deterministic logic from AI decision layers
- Add validation before scaling automation
- Design for failure and recovery, not just success
- Eliminate manual work, but not necessary human judgment
- Avoid automation debt by documenting and standardizing early

---

### AI Workflow Design Model

**Deterministic Layer → AI Layer → Validation Layer → Output Layer**

**Deterministic Layer:** Rules, triggers, compliance logic. Ensures consistency and control.

**AI Layer:** Classification, enrichment, personalization. Adds flexibility and intelligence.

**Validation Layer:** Guards against hallucination and errors. Ensures output reliability.

**Output Layer:** Structured data, CRM updates, routing, alerts. Must be immediately usable by teams.
