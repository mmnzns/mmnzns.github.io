In one of our recent issues ([read more](https://www.directtoconsumer.co/newsletter/how-brands-can-leverage-deinfluencing-trend)) at [DTC Newsletter](https://www.linkedin.com/company/dtc-mediateam/), the team delved into "The Power of Deinfluencing," and it got me thinking about how Gen Z has been bombarded by ads from every angle their entire lives.

As a millennial, I had the privilege of a more ad-light childhood. Sure, we had commercials, but nothing like the constant digital barrage Gen Z experiences — from mobile games on their parents' phones to YouTube videos and TikTok. Deinfluencing offers much-needed relief from this overwhelming marketing landscape, providing consumers with honest opinions to guide their purchasing decisions.

## The rise and fall of influencing

Back in the early 2010s, micro-influencers started to gain fame on their platforms, creating a new marketing strategy for brands. It was fresh and exciting, but fast forward to now, and the influencer space is oversaturated. This has led to some serious fatigue among consumers, who are tired of the endless product pushes and promotions.

## So what's deinfluencing?

Deinfluencing is all about counteracting the overwhelming influencer culture. It's a movement where people share honest opinions about viral products to help others avoid impulse buys that lead to disappointment. The hashtag #deinfluencing has blown up on TikTok, racking up over a billion views. People are becoming more skeptical of influencer claims and craving authenticity and transparency.

## Why are people loving deinfluencing?

There are a few reasons why deinfluencing is catching on:

1. **Skepticism towards materialism.** More and more, people are aware of sustainability and want genuine recommendations, not just sales pitches.
2. **Economic consciousness.** With the economic fallout from the pandemic, consumers are more mindful about their spending and seek trustworthy sources for honest reviews.
3. **Influencer fatigue.** Many influencers have lost credibility by prioritizing sales over sincerity. Deinfluencers are valued for their honest, sometimes critical, takes on products.

## Meet the deinfluencers

Deinfluencers have emerged as the new go-to voices for many consumers, empowering them with their critical attitude, transparency, and authenticity. They stand out because:

- **Critical attitude:** they question the need and benefits of products, often pointing out their flaws and suggesting better, cheaper, or more sustainable alternatives.
- **Transparency:** they share personal, honest experiences without the bias of sponsorships.
- **Authenticity:** their content is genuine, making them more relatable and trustworthy.

## The role of social media

Social media platforms have been crucial in the rise of deinfluencing. TikTok, in particular, has become a hub for deinfluencing content. With its short-form video format, users can quickly share their thoughts and experiences, reaching a vast audience almost instantly. This ease of sharing and the potential for virality has made deinfluencing a powerful force on social media.

## The economic factor

The economic impact of the global pandemic cannot be overlooked. With rising inflation rates and the cost of living increasing, consumers are cutting back on unnecessary spending. Deinfluencing aligns with this economic reality, encouraging people to think critically about their purchases and prioritize what they truly need over what is trendy.

## Environmental and ethical considerations

The trend towards deinfluencing is also driven by a growing awareness of environmental and ethical issues. Consumers are increasingly concerned about overconsumption and the environmental impact of their buying habits. Deinfluencers often highlight these concerns, promoting more sustainable and ethical consumption practices.

## Implications for brands

Brands need to adapt to this shift by:

- **Embracing honesty.** Brands should recognize the importance of honest feedback, even if it's not always positive. This transparency is key to building credibility and trust with their audience.
- **Building credibility.** Long-term, genuine partnerships with deinfluencers can enhance a brand's authenticity.
- **Leveraging feedback.** Insights from deinfluencing content can be invaluable for product improvement and addressing consumer needs.

Brands that ignore the deinfluencing trend risk losing credibility and consumer trust. By aligning with deinfluencers, brands can demonstrate their commitment to transparency and build stronger, more authentic connections with their audience.

## Wrapping up

Deinfluencing represents a shift towards more conscious consumption and authentic marketing. Does this mean gone are the days when big-budget ads and aggressive promotions from influencers were the norm? No, I don't think that will be the case. With TikTok's launch of their e-commerce platform, TikTok Shop, I suspect that influencers will be on the rise again. Rather than just representing brands, more and more influencers will now be repping their own products to sell on their pages.

But what this shows is that a chunk of consumers are becoming more discerning. Brands and influencers must pivot towards genuine engagement and transparency. There is now a market for people who crave the human touch in brands, and social media has made it easier for consumers to share their experiences. Authentic marketing is on the rise, helping to build deeper, more trustful connections with audiences. This approach fosters a more sustainable and credible marketplace.
